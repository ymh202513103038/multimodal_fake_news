# Maricopa County didn't apologize to Trump after uncovering fraud in 2020 election, despite claims

Claim:
Maricopa County Elections in Arizona issued an apology to U.S. President Donald Trump after officials presented evidence showing state leaders ordered thousands of Trump voters' ballots in the 2020 election to be destroyed and also to count immigrants' illegally cast votes for Joe Biden.
Rating:
False
About this rating
Ahead of the 2026 mideterm elections, people shared a purported "breaking" rumor alleging that Maricopa County Elections in Arizona issued an apology to President Donald Trump after officials made a striking discovery regarding the 2020 election results. The final tally showed Trump losing Arizona to former President Joe Biden
by just under 11,000 votes
.
According to the rumor, officials presented evidence that Gov. Katie Hobbs, a Democrat who in 2020 was Arizona's secretary of state, and former Arizona House Speaker Rusty Bowers, a Republican, ordered the destruction of tens of thousands of Trump voters' ballots and also to count of tens of thousands of votes that immigrants lacking permanent legal status illegally cast to help elect Biden.
Snopes readers asked about this matter via email and searches.
This rumor was false. Searches for proof failed to find any credible sources reporting about Maricopa County Elections issuing an apology to Trump about large-scale voter fraud in the 2020 election. Instead, those queries indicated the original post came from a user who has shared similar misinformation before, including a false rumor about Georgia's 2020 election results.
People sharing this story copied and pasted the same text, which touched on Trump's false, oft-repeated claim that officials engaged in massive election fraud in 2020 — supposedly resulting in a
"stolen election."
The Associated Press extensively investigated Trump's claims involving battleground states and found
no evidence of any large-scale fraud
.
We emailed Maricopa County Elections t
o ask if the office could confirm that it did not issue an apology to Trump. We also emailed the White House to ask about this rumor and will update this article if we receive any further information.

'Unsupported by factual evidence'
In early March — just days before Hall's X post about Maricopa County — the FBI reportedly
subpoenaed 2020 elections records
to continue investigating Trump's unfounded claims of fraud.
Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes, a Democrat,
issued a statement in response
, repeating details of elections officials' exhaustive investigations and calling the claims "unsupported by factual evidence":
The 2020 General Election in Arizona has been exhaustively reviewed. Multiple audits, court proceedings, and independent investigations — including those pursued by members of the same political party of the President — found no evidence of fraud sufficient to alter the outcome. These findings are not in dispute by any credible authority. The election results were certified, litigated, and affirmed.
In fact, under the direction of my predecessor, Attorney General Mark Brnovich, agents and support staff of the Arizona Attorney General's Office spent over 10,000 hours
investigating
voting irregularities and alleged instances of illegal voting by high-profile election deniers. Complaints and allegations submitted to the Arizona Attorney General's Office by members of the public were also unsupported by factual evidence.
On Oct. 5, Trump
criticized Maricopa County elections officials
for sending out more than 3,000 ballots six days early. The Maricopa County Recorder's Office
issued a statement
saying that an election services company mistakenly mailed the ballots early, that the error "affected only the timing of the mailing" and that "the correct ballots were mailed to the correct voters."
