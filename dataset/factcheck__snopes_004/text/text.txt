# Are videos of Tesla's new 'flying' car performing mid-air stunts real?

Claim:
Electric vehicle manufacturer Tesla posted videos on Oct. 15 authentically showing the company's new "flying" car performing mid-air stunts.
Rating:
True
About this rating
Context
Tesla posted the videos to showcase the official launch of the company's new Roadster model. The footage shows the car performing several mid-air stunts in front of a live audience. Attendees also posted their own videos from the event, corroborating Tesla's footage. Whether the vehicle actually "flew" during the demonstration is up to individual interpretation. It is unclear whether Tesla's final product would have the same capabilities as the "flying" car seen in the clips.
On Oct. 15, electric vehicle manufacturer Tesla and social media users posted videos purportedly showing the company's new "flying" car performing a number of mid-air stunts.
The footage depicts a launch event for the automaker's long-awaited
Roadster
model. It shows a prototype
driving up
an upside-down ramp before flipping off and landing on its wheels,
flipping

accelerating
from zero to 60 mph in 0.81 seconds.
Throughout the video, Tesla's CEO, Elon Musk, and his fellow hosts made references to the vehicle being a "flying car."

In short, there is nothing to suggest the video is fake, meaning it was not created using artificial intelligence tools or the product of digital editing. Tesla livestreamed the footage on YouTube on Oct. 15. The event showcased the company's new Roadster model and was conducted in front of a live audience. Some of those attendees posted their own clips on social media, corroborating Tesla's footage. Therefore, we rate this claim true.

Nearly nine years after it was announced, Musk

According to Tesla's
website
, the automaker would sell two versions of the Roadster. The "Thrust" would feature "a multi-mode suction system paired with cold-gas thrusters" that allow the car to accelerate from zero to 60 mph in under one second. The "Rocket," which members of the public would be able to buy via invitation only, would be the flying version of the car, fitted with cold-gas thrusters that "support forward and upward acceleration as well as lateral rotational movement."
Tesla says of the "Rocket" version, "So yes, it can fly."
The company notes on its website that the "Rocket" is "not approved for public road use." It is unclear at the time of publication how a flying car would be regulated and what sort of licenses a driver or pilot might need to hold to operate it.
